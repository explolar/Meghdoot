MEGHDOOT

Hyper-local severe-rainfall early warning for eastern India, 0–3 hours ahead, at 4 km, from satellite alone.

Ministry of Earth Sciences · Disaster Management

Public shell. The live map and forecast layers arrive with the product stage.

The problem

Most of eastern India has no weather radar. Warnings are issued for whole districts, hours late, while the flooding happens street by street.

Rain is the disaster mechanism here: waterlogging, drainage failure and transport shutdown. A warning that names a district and a day cannot be acted on. One that names a 4 km cell and a time can.

The approach

India already has the observation it needs. INSAT-3DR sees every village every 30 minutes at 4 km. We forecast the next three hours from that image stream, then turn the forecast into a calibrated warning.

4 kmcell size, not districts
0–3 hforecast horizon
30 minrefresh, day and night
₹0external data cost

The model is a U-Net with a residual head and one output head per lead time, pretrained on twenty years of GPM IMERG and fine-tuned on INSAT-3DR. It is scored every cycle against persistence, climatology, optical flow and ISRO’s own operational nowcast.

What we are careful about

  • Warning versus guidance. 0–60 minutes is a warning. 60–180 minutes is a probability at 20 km scale, and is labelled that way.
  • The ceiling is published. At the highest rain rates every method in the literature scores close to zero. We report that rather than forecast through it.
  • Named exclusions. This forecasts rainfall. Lightning, hail and wind gusts each need an instrument we cannot obtain, so we do not claim them.
  • Impact, not flood prediction. The impact layer ranks locations by exposure. It is not a hydraulic model.
